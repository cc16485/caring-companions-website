Questions families ask

Is the assessment free? Do we have to decide that day?

Yes, it's free, and there's no obligation. We listen, answer your questions and leave you everything in writing. Take the time you need.

How much does care cost?

$30, $32 or $34 an hour, depending on the level of care: Wellness Care, Personal Care or Advanced Care. We'll recommend the right level together at the visit. Your rate is written in your agreement, and we give at least 4 weeks' written notice before any change.

Is there a deposit or a long-term contract?

No deposit and no long-term contract. You're billed weekly, after the visits have happened, from your caregiver's actual clock-in and clock-out times. You can stop care when you need to.

What is the difference between Wellness, Personal and Advanced Care?

Wellness Care ($30 an hour) is companionship, meals, light housekeeping, errands and medication reminders, for someone who does not need hands-on help. Personal Care ($32) adds bathing, dressing, toileting, walking and safe transfers. Advanced Care ($34) is for advanced dementia, serious mobility limits or other needs that call for more experienced caregivers. Compare the care levels

Do you offer 24-hour care or overnight care?

Yes. For 24-hour care, caregivers work 8- or 12-hour rotating shifts so someone is always there. Overnight care is one caregiver from evening to morning, usually an 8- to 12-hour shift. The schedule we can offer depends on your loved one's needs, the in-home assessment and caregiver availability, so we tell you honestly what we can staff before care starts. Typical rates are $30 to $34 an hour. 24-hour care · Overnight care

Is there a minimum?

Our standard visit is at least 4 hours, with at least 8 hours a week. Need a shorter visit? Please ask. When a caregiver is available for a shorter shift, we will work to make it happen, with an extra $5 an hour for visits under 4 hours. Shorter visits depend on staffing, so we cannot promise them every time.

How soon can care start?

It depends on the schedule you need and finding the right caregiver for your loved one. We don't rush the match. Once you're ready, we go over your Client Care Agreement together, set up payment and match your caregiver by skills, personality and schedule. Then we call you to confirm the day, the time and who's coming. At your assessment, we'll give you an honest idea of timing for your schedule.

Can in-home care start today or tomorrow?

Sometimes, yes. We understand some families need help urgently, and we always try to move quickly, so care can sometimes begin the same day or next day. Before the first caregiver shift, we must complete an in-home assessment, build the care plan with you and confirm a caregiver is available, so we cannot promise an immediate start. If your situation is urgent, tell us on the first call. Request a free assessment

Will we know who's coming?

Always. Before the first visit, we send your caregiver's profile with a friendly photo. If your caregiver ever changes, the family members you choose get a text with the new caregiver's profile.

What if our caregiver isn't a good fit?

Tell us. We call a few days after the first visit to see how it's going, and you can call us any time before then. Most things can be worked out with a quick change to the care plan. If it still isn't right, we'll do our best to send a different caregiver. We aim to keep the same small group of caregivers with your loved one, so it's worth getting the match right.

What happens if the caregiver is sick?

Your caregiver calls the office, and we let you know right away. We'll do our best to send a replacement. If you asked for text updates, the people in your Family Circle get a text with a link to the new caregiver's profile, so you're never surprised by who's at the door. A real person answers our office line, day or night: (417) 234-8494.

Are your caregivers bonded and insured?

Yes. Our caregivers are bonded and insured, including when they drive clients. We carry professional liability, general liability, non-owned auto, employment practices and workers' compensation insurance.

Do you do background checks?

Yes, on every caregiver and every office team member, before they start: federal, state and county background checks, a driving record check, and verification that they're allowed to work. The checks are repeated over time, not just done once.

Are caregivers your employees?

Always. Every caregiver is a Caring Companions employee, never a contractor. We handle their payroll and all their taxes, so you never become anyone's employer. Every caregiver also has at least a year of paid caregiving experience and finishes our orientation before their first visit with a client.

Can a caregiver drive Mom to appointments?

Yes, in your car or theirs. There's no mileage charge in your car. In the caregiver's car, it's $0.69 a mile.

What can't caregivers do?

We're non-medical home care. Caregivers remind about medicines but can't give them, fill pill organizers, apply creams or drops, or check blood sugar. A family member can fill the pill organizer, and we'll help you find a solution for anything beyond that.

How will we know how the visits are going?

Caregivers write a note after every visit, and our office reads every one. If a caregiver hasn't clocked in a few minutes after a visit should start, we're alerted right away. The family members you choose can see the calendar, notes and invoices in AxisCare.

Does insurance or the VA help pay?

Depending on the policy, we may be able to bill long-term care insurance directly, and we work with VA benefits and Medicare GUIDE (respite visits Medicare pays for, if you qualify). If your loved one has active Medicaid, our sister company Caring Companions Consumer Directed Services can help: caringcds.com or (417) 218-2888.

Will long-term care insurance pay for in-home care?

Many long-term care policies help pay for in-home care, but what is covered depends on your own policy. We review your policy for free, can join a call with your insurance company, and help you with the claim forms. Depending on your policy and insurer, we may be able to bill them directly, or give you paid invoices to send in yourself. Long-term care insurance

Does Medicaid pay for in-home care in Missouri?

Yes, through our sister agency, Caring Companions CDS. Missouri's Consumer Directed Services (CDS) program is consumer-directed: the person receiving care chooses their own caregiver, called an attendant, and directs that care. Medicaid pays the attendant for the services the state approves. The attendant can be a family member or friend, but not a spouse or legal guardian. Visit caringcds.com or call 417-218-2888.

Does the VA help pay for in-home care?

There are two different VA benefits. VA Community Care is care the VA arranges and pays for through approved providers, for veterans who qualify. Aid & Attendance is a monthly pension payment to eligible veterans or surviving spouses, which families can put toward care costs. Many families use both, and an accredited Veterans Service Officer helps with the paperwork for free. Our VA benefits guide

Yes. Our Client Handbook and a sample Client Care Agreement are below on this page. They're the same documents our clients get.

Who's coming to the assessment, and how long does it take?

One of our care coordinators comes to the home. Your confirmation shows who, with a link to their profile, so you'll know who's at the door. The visit usually takes 60 to 90 minutes.

What areas do you serve?

Our home counties are Greene, Christian, Webster and Polk, where our caregivers live and work. We also serve Taney, Lawrence, Dallas and Stone counties when a qualified caregiver is nearby. Call (417) 234-8494 and we will tell you where things stand. Service areas

Do you help people living with dementia?

We provide personalized support for people living with dementia, including help with routines, daily activities, personal care and companionship. Advanced Care is available for more complex needs, subject to an in-home assessment and appropriate staffing. Dementia and memory care

At your visit

A free visit to get to know your loved one.

One of our care coordinators comes to the home. Your confirmation shows who, with a link to their profile, so you know who's at the door. Plan on 60 to 90 minutes. Anyone you'd like there is welcome, and your loved one's voice comes first.

What we'll talk about

  1. Their story. Their routines, what they enjoy, and what's gotten harder lately.
  2. Health and medicines. Conditions, allergies and the medicines they take, so the care plan is safe.
  3. Everyday help. What they do on their own, and where a hand would help: meals, bathing, dressing, getting around.
  4. Who they'd like as a caregiver. Preferences like pets, smoking, language or a man or a woman.
  5. Schedule, level of care and cost. The days and hours that fit, which level of care is right, and what a week would cost.
  6. Who should stay in the loop. The family members you'd like in your Family Circle.

What you'll have when we leave

  • A personalized care proposal in writing: the level we recommend and why, the days and hours, and the estimated weekly cost.
  • Your own Client Handbook.
  • A what happens next sheet, so the steps are clear.

There's no obligation and no decision needed that day. Talk it over with your family. If you're ready, you can sign your Client Care Agreement at the visit, or later from home.

Good to have handy

  • A list of medications
  • Doctors' names and numbers
  • Insurance, long-term care policy or VA details
  • Any power of attorney or advance directive papers
  • Who you'd want in your Family Circle

When needs change

Your care grows with you, without changing agencies. If more hands-on help is needed, we can move you from Wellness Care to Personal Care or Advanced Care. If things get easier, we can step back down. Add hours, add overnight care when nights are the worry, or move to 24-hour care when someone needs to be there all the time.

Going into the hospital or rehab

Please call the office as soon as you can so we can pause visits. Before care starts again, we'll check the care plan with you, because needs often change after a hospital stay. If a family caregiver needs time off, respite visits can fill the gap, scheduled or as needed.

Non-medical home care

What caregivers can and cannot do.

Our caregivers can assist with daily activities, mobility, and medication reminders, but cannot administer medications, fill pill organizers, change dressings, or perform nursing procedures. For everyone's safety, caregivers cannot perform unsafe lifting, move heavy furniture, climb ladders or stools, or complete outdoor maintenance. All assistance must follow your care plan.

Working together

A few things that keep care running smoothly.

The everyday rules our clients and caregivers follow. Tap any one to read it.

Schedule changes go through the office, whether it's one visit or a permanent change. We'll let your caregiver know.
Caregivers don't give out their personal phone numbers. The office can always reach them for you.
Asking a caregiver to stay late? Call the office first so we can approve it.
Severe weather: we never ask caregivers to take unnecessary risks, and we'll call if a visit may not happen.
Outings with a cost: please pay for your caregiver's admission as well as your own, plus any parking or tolls.
Checks and money: a caregiver may help you write a check, but will never sign one. Cash for shopping always comes back with a receipt and your exact change.

Your caregiver can't be hired privately, even for a few extra hours. Every caregiver is our employee, and your agreement includes a $5,000 direct-hire fee.

Your rights, your privacy, and how to raise a concern.

Among them: to be treated with dignity and respect, to help create your care plan, to know what your care costs and be told in advance of any increase, and to make a complaint without fear of retaliation. All 18 rights are in the Client Handbook.

Call the office any time, or write to us. Every concern goes to the agency administrator. We look into it right away, tell you what we found, and your care continues as normal while we work it out. Still not right? Call Samantha directly.
