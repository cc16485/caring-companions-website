Always announced, never a hidden camera

A check-in is a call, the same as one from you. It comes up on the screen and she knows exactly who is there. Your family chooses in the settings whether calls answer automatically or whether she presses the button on her remote, and that choice is yours to change any time.

Who it is for

You qualify if all three are true

  • 20 or more hours of care a week. That is roughly three hours a day, or two longer visits.
  • Private pay. Including long-term care insurance and VA benefits paid privately. Medicaid-funded care is not eligible for this offer.
  • Thirty days of service. The device ships once care is up and running, so it arrives to a settled household.

The honest fine print

The equipment stays ours and comes back to us when care ends, the same as any loaned equipment. Getting it back is all we ask. There is a $300 charge only if it is never returned.

If care drops below 20 hours a week for two months running, we will ask whether you would like to keep it at the regular $99 a month or send it back. Nobody gets a surprise bill.

Internet in the home is required. It plugs into an HDMI port on almost any television made in the last fifteen years.

Why the virtual visits are the quiet bargain

An in-person visit anywhere in the country runs about $130 once you count the four-hour minimum most agencies insist on. A caregiver checking in on her TV every weekday starts at $75 for the whole week, and you can have them come once, twice or three times a day. It will never replace someone in the room, and it is not meant to. But for the days between visits, it is the cheapest way there is to have real eyes on her.
