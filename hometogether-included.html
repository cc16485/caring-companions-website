Every worry, and the thing that answers it

You can read a care note and still not know how your mother sounded today. Families tell us the same thing constantly: the care is good, and they still lie awake. Nobody wants another device in the house. What they want is one less thing to be afraid of, so here is what each piece is actually for.

“I'm afraid she's lonely.”

Family video calls on her TV

Up to ten people on one call, and she can be set to answer automatically. No swiping, no squinting at a phone, no asking the caregiver to work a tablet for her. Grandchildren in three states on a Sunday afternoon, on a screen big enough for her to actually see their faces.

“I'm afraid she'll forget her medications.”

Reminders on screen

Medication times, appointments, and the days you are coming appear on the television at the moment they matter, in the room she is already sitting in. Not a note on the counter she walks past.

“I'm afraid something will happen when nobody is there.”

Quiet safety sensing

Motion and environment sensing runs in the background and notices when a normal day stops looking normal. It does not use the camera, and the camera it does have, for calls, sits behind a physical shutter she can close with her thumb. Nobody watches a live feed of your mother.

“I'm afraid the caregiver leaves and nobody knows what's happening.”

You can look in yourself, any hour

Evenings, weekends, the gaps between visits. You call, her TV lights up, and you see her instead of hearing “I'm fine.” If you would rather someone from our office look in on the days no caregiver is scheduled, virtual visits start at $75 a week.

“I'm afraid I'm missing something important.”

The whole family sees the same thing

Everyone you approve gets the same updates, so one adult child is not the family switchboard, repeating the day to four siblings every night. Changes get noticed by the people who know her, not just by us.

Everything it actually does

Families ask us what is in the box, so here is the whole thing. She only ever touches the television and the remote. Everything else happens on your phone or quietly in the background.

On her television

The only part she has to learn, and there is almost nothing to learn.

  • Video calls with the family. A wide-angle HD camera built into the hub, up to ten people on a call, and it can be set to answer automatically so she never has to press a thing.
  • Photos and videos you send. Anything the family shares plays right on her TV. Grandchildren's ball games and vacation pictures land on a screen she can see, not a phone she has to hold.
  • Activity channels made for her. Programming chosen for older adults, on the same screen, on the same remote.
  • Medication and appointment reminders. They appear on screen at the time they matter, in the room she is already sitting in.
  • One simple remote. No new box to figure out, no smartphone, no tablet, no password.

Quiet safety, running in the background

None of this uses the camera. It watches for patterns, not for pictures.

  • Motion sensing in the rooms that matter, so a day that stops looking like her normal day gets noticed.
  • Room temperature and humidity. A furnace that quits in January or an air conditioner that dies in July shows up before she thinks to mention it.
  • Ambient light sensing, which tells you a great deal about whether someone got up, opened the curtains, and started their day.
  • Sound detection that can pick out sounds worth knowing about without anybody listening in.
  • A physical camera shutter that closes over the lens, so you can see with your own eyes when the camera is off.

Health readings, if you want them

Optional. Plenty of families never turn this on, and that is fine.

  • The hub connects to home health devices over its built-in connection, so readings do not have to be written on a notepad.
  • Blood pressure, blood sugar, weight, temperature, and pulse oximetry from compatible devices.
  • Readings sync to the family portal automatically, so the numbers are already there when the doctor asks.
  • Ask us which devices before you buy any. We will tell you what pairs and what does not.

For you, on your phone

The family app, on iPhone and Android, plus a web portal.

  • Call her from anywhere, and see her instead of hearing that she is fine.
  • Set her reminders and tasks yourself, from your kitchen in another state.
  • A dashboard showing how things have been going, not just a snapshot of this minute.
  • Manage who can call her. Only the contacts you approve can reach her television.
  • Roles for everyone involved. Family, friends, and caregivers each get their own level of access, so a neighbor who calls on Sundays does not see her blood pressure.

The parts you should never have to think about

We set it up, we keep it running, and we are the ones you call.

  • No broadband at the house? It can run on the cellular network instead. Ask us, because a good number of homes out in the county do not have decent internet and this is the whole reason that matters.
  • We install it. Somebody from our office brings it out and sets it up in her living room. Nothing arrives in a box for you to figure out.
  • Software updates happen on their own, remotely, without anybody bothering her about it.
  • Repair and replacement are handled through us if a piece of it ever fails.
  • You call our Springfield office, not a manufacturer's support line in another time zone.
  • It leaves with the care. If care ends, we come pick it up. There is nothing to cancel and nothing to ship back.
