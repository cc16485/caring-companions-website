You call. She says she's fine.

The things families ask us first

Is it really free, or is it built into the price?
Really free. Our rates are the same whether you take the device or not: around $30 an hour for help around the house, $32 for personal care, $34 for advanced care. Nothing is added to your bill for it, and there is no contract for the device itself.
What if we do not need 20 hours a week?
Then you have three choices and none of them is a dead end. Add a day and reach 20. Keep your hours and buy the device on its own at $99 a month. Or add virtual caregiver visits from $75 a week, where a caregiver appears on her television once, twice or three times a day, Monday through Friday. The assessment above will tell you which one fits.
Is there a camera watching her?
There is a camera, because that is how she sees your face. It sits behind a shutter that physically closes when a call ends. The safety sensing uses radar rather than video, so nothing is watching her between calls. Your family also chooses whether calls answer automatically or whether she presses the button on her remote.
What does the care itself cost?
Around $30 an hour for help around the house, $32 for personal care, and $34 for advanced or bed-bound care. Our shortest visit is 4 hours. Live-in care is charged by the day at $450 rather than by the hour. The assessment gives you a range in about a minute and a coordinator confirms the real number with you.
Does she need to be good with technology?
No, and that is rather the point. There is no tablet, no app on her end and no password. It is the television she has used for forty years. Most of the setup is done before it reaches her, and it plugs in with one cable.
Do you take Medicaid?
Yes, we serve Medicaid HCBS clients. The free device offer is for private-pay families only, which includes long-term care insurance and privately paid VA benefits.
What if she says she does not want anyone in the house?
Almost everybody says that at first. It is not stubbornness, it is dignity. We start small, often with something practical like a ride or the laundry rather than personal care, and we let her get to know one person. The device helps here too, because it gives her something rather than only taking her independence.
What happens to the device if care ends?
It comes back to us, the same as any loaned equipment, and that is all we ask. There is a $300 charge only if it is never returned. If your hours drop below 20 for two months running, we will ask whether you would like to keep it at $99 a month or send it back. Nobody gets a surprise bill.
