Why it matters

The riskiest moments of the day are the most private ones.

Getting in and out of the shower, up from the toilet, or out of bed are where many falls happen. A steady hand at those moments can be the difference between staying home and a hospital stay.

1 in 4adults 65 and older fall each year. Falls are the leading cause of injury at that age.
1 in 2bathroom injuries in people 85 and older happen on or near the toilet.
Mosthip fractures in older adults are caused by falls.
3 in 4adults 50 and older want to stay in their own home as they age.

Sources: CDC, older adult falls; CDC MMWR, bathroom injuries, 2011; CDC MMWR, falls and hip fractures; AARP, 2024.

How we do it

Done with them, not to them.

Needing help with a shower is hard for anyone. The way the help is given matters as much as the help itself.

They lead

Their routine, their choices

Morning or evening shower, which outfit, how they like their hair. We follow their way of doing things.

They do what they can

Help only where it's needed

If they can button the shirt, they button the shirt. Doing for themselves keeps strength and confidence up.

Privacy, always

Covered, doors closed, no rush

We keep them covered, explain each step before we do it, and never hurry the most personal parts of the day.

Safe moves

Transfers, walking, the bathroom

Caregivers help them stand, turn and sit safely, and use the walker, shower chair or grab bars they have.

A familiar face

The same caregivers when we can

Trust is what makes personal care feel normal. We try hard to send the same people, so it's someone they know.

Small changes noticed

A note after every visit

A red spot on the skin, eating less, a new wobble. A caregiver who helps every day sees what others miss.

A real visit

What a morning can look like.

One example of a Personal Care visit for a dad who uses a walker.

7:30Good morning. Help sitting up and getting out of bed safely.
7:45A warm shower on the shower chair, with help washing his back and feet.
8:15Dressed in the shirt he picked, hair combed, ready for the day.
8:45Breakfast together and a reminder for his morning medicine.
9:30Fresh sheets, a load of laundry, and the kitchen cleaned up.
10:30A walk down the block with his walker, or a game of cards if it's raining.
11:30Lunch made and ready, and a short note about how the morning went.

Signs it may be time

You might be noticing:

Fewer showers, or the same clothes several days in a row

A fall or a close call in the bathroom or getting out of bed

Holding onto furniture to get across the room

Trouble with buttons, shoes or reaching the feet

Embarrassing accidents they try to hide

A family member worn out from lifting and bathing

Seeing one or two of these is a good reason for a free in-home assessment. We'll tell you honestly whether Wellness Care is enough or Personal Care is the better fit.
